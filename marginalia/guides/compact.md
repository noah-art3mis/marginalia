# Compact a draft

Agree on the desired length or density and which content is protected. Read the whole draft to understand repetition and dependencies before proposing local changes. Compaction may reduce the space devoted to ideas, but does not authorize removing them without approval.

Distinguish wording compression from information cuts. Combining sentences can preserve both claims; deleting an example, caveat, repeated substantive claim, or supporting detail changes what the draft contains. Declare those cuts and obtain separate explicit approval. Do not treat “compact this” as blanket authorization for omissions.

Submit manageable proposals with original text, replacement, reason, and any declared cuts. Explain when a local proposal depends on another passage so the author can judge the resulting argument. Do not remove repeated material until you identify what remains and where. Use `marginalia guide review` for acceptance, rejection, revision, and outdated targets.
