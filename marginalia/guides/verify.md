# Verify references and assertions

Read the whole draft and the active brief before checking anything. A verify pass tests whether the draft's references support what they are cited for and whether its assertions are true. It does not change voice, argument, or structure. Every finding becomes an author query or a small replacement proposal whose explanation names the source consulted; nothing is corrected silently.

## References

For each link, citation, named author, and named source, open the target or read the source text. Check that the target exists, that the title and author the draft gives match it, and that the cited passage says what the draft attributes to it. Check numbers against the data, notebook, or tables the author points to, and against each other where the draft repeats them. A reference you could not reach is reported as unreached, not as wrong.

## Assertions

Hunt statements asserted as fact: definitions, attributions, generalizations, historical and causal claims, and figures with no source. Give each a verdict with its evidence: supported, contested, false, or unverifiable. For a false or contested claim, propose a replacement that keeps the author's point in an accurate form, or ask a query when the accurate form changes the argument. Leave the author's hedges and opinions as written; an opinion is not a false assertion.

## Report

Work in passages. For each, list the claim, verdict, evidence, and proposed action, and separate what you checked from what you inferred. Record the author's decisions through `marginalia guide review`; a claim the author marks as deliberate is not raised again in this pass.
