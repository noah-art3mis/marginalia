# Deduplicate information

Duplication is the same information stated in more than one place: a repeated sentence, a figure given twice, a point made in the body and again in a summary that adds nothing. Read the whole draft and list every duplicate pair with both locations before proposing, since pairs that sit sections apart are missed by local reading.

For each pair decide which occurrence stays, usually the one where the reader first needs it or the fuller one. A deliberate restatement, such as a conclusion, a callback, or a running joke, is repetition with a purpose; keep it and ask when unsure. When each occurrence carries a detail the other lacks, the replacement keeps both details. Removing an occurrence is an information cut only when it carried something absent from the one kept; declare it as such.

Identical passages break the uniqueness of `original`, so anchor the proposal with a neighbouring line. When the structure pass has placed duplicates side by side, the merge is one proposal. When they are apart, prefer removing the later one and check that references such as "as above" still resolve. After acceptance, re-read the surviving occurrence in place, since a removed repetition may have carried a transition.
