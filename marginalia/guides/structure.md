# Propose headings and reorganize passages

Ask what the draft should help its reader understand. Read the whole draft before suggesting an outline so passages near the end are considered. Present proposed headings and their purpose in the terminal conversation; obtain approval of the outline before moving passages.

Classify existing passages under approved headings without rewriting their body text. Preserve claims, examples, uncertainty, links, citations, punctuation, and wording. Do not summarize, deduplicate, translate, or quietly discard material during classification. New headings are separate proposed additions requiring approval. Use an `insert` proposal with a unique existing passage as `original` and the heading plus separators as `replacement`; acceptance inserts the heading before the anchor and retains the anchor unchanged.

Show each proposed move with its source, destination, and exact unchanged passage. Submit moves through Marginalia rather than rebuilding the document with direct writes. For mixed paragraphs, discuss a split at existing sentence boundaries and preserve all original characters across the resulting passages. If the current CLI cannot express a split safely, leave the text unchanged and explain the limitation.

Ask an author query when a passage fits several sections or its intended role is unclear. Work through manageable portions. After accepted moves, inspect the resulting document and verify that every original body passage remains present. A content-preserving move can still be editorially misplaced, so mechanical text checks do not replace review of the organization.

Once the author approves the structure, finish the structuring pass through Versions. Start prose editing as a separate pass so reorganizing text does not silently become rewriting it.
